01 — What Netso believes Position

Four convictions that shape everything we build.

01

The constraint is ownership, not technology.

Solar generates reliably and the economics of a good rooftop project are well understood. What holds the market back is that someone has to put capital into an asset on land they do not own and be accountable for it for twenty years. Netso exists to be that someone.

02

Distributed generation is infrastructure, not a product.

A rooftop system that is designed, financed, maintained and insured for its full life is a small power station with a contracted customer. Treated that way, it can be underwritten, financed and repeated. Treated as an equipment sale, it usually is not.

03

The customer should buy what they actually want.

Commercial and industrial consumers want cheaper, more predictable electricity. They do not want to become power plant operators. Selling kilowatt-hours instead of equipment aligns the developer’s revenue with the asset’s performance.

04

Behind the meter is where the value is.

Generation consumed on the same site avoids transmission, distribution and losses, and competes directly with the tariff the customer is already paying. The shortest distance between a generator and its customer is the roof they share.

02 — Why Bangladesh Market

One of the most industrialised economies in South Asia, with one of the largest untapped rooftop inventories.

Bangladesh’s manufacturing base is concentrated, energy-intensive and expanding — and most of it sits under large, flat, load-bearing roofs.

Industrial and commercial consumers carry the cost of a grid under strain. Electricity tariffs for business have risen consistently, fuel supply for captive generation is uncertain, and reliability matters more than ever to operations that cannot stop.

At the same time, an increasing share of export-facing industry has to report and reduce the carbon intensity of its production to stay competitive with buyers in Europe and North America. Onsite generation addresses both the cost line and the reporting line at once.

Bangladesh is also a market where the physical prerequisites for distributed infrastructure are unusually favourable: dense industrial clusters, a single national grid and regulatory framework, and facility owners who already operate their own generation and understand the economics of power.

Evidence

Market figures — installed capacity, tariff levels, rooftop-area estimates — are published only where each can be sourced and dated, and they carry the source when they are. Where we do not have a citable number, the site leaves the gap rather than filling it with an estimate of our own.

03 — Why distributed C&I energy Thesis

Generation at the point of consumption is the shortest, cheapest and least contested route to new capacity.

Land

No land required

Rooftop generation uses an existing surface. It does not compete for land, and it needs no new rights-of-way through populated areas.

Speed

Faster to build

A behind-the-meter installation is delivered in a fraction of the time of utility-scale generation, with a much smaller permitting footprint.

Economics

Competes with retail tariff

The alternative to onsite generation is the price the customer already pays for grid electricity — not a wholesale price.

Grid

Relieves the network

Every unit generated and consumed onsite is a unit that does not have to be delivered through an increasingly constrained distribution network.

04 — The asset-owner thesis Why it can scale

A portfolio of contracted, metered, physical assets in one market.

Every Netso project is structured to produce the same three things: contracted revenue, an asset that stays under our ownership, and data about how that asset performs.

Contracted revenue
A long-term PPA with a commercial counterparty consuming the electricity on site
Physical asset
Equipment with a measured output, an insured value and a defined maintenance regime
Repeatable development
A process that can be applied to the next roof, district and customer
Portfolio effect
Diversified counterparties, sites and load profiles, aggregated under one operator

This combination is what makes distributed generation financeable beyond a single project. Infrastructure capital is comfortable with assets that are built, owned, contracted and operated to a standard — and uncomfortable with one-off installations that no single party owns or maintains.

Netso is designed around that discipline from the first project rather than as an afterthought once a pipeline exists. Ownership forces it: we cannot originate a project, sell it, and walk away. We hold the consequence of how it performs.

That is also why our roadmap moves from solar to storage, then to energy management, and eventually to a coordinated network of distributed assets. Each step increases the value of the same underlying position: ownership of generating capacity at the customer’s meter.

06 — Origin

Why Netso is built around ownership.

Netso Energy Ltd. was built around a specific problem: Bangladesh’s commercial and industrial facilities pay for electricity that is expensive, increasingly carbon-intensive to source from the grid, and less reliable than their operations require — while the large flat roofs above those same operations sit empty.

Distributed generation was the obvious answer, and it was already being sold in the market. But it was being sold as equipment: to facility owners who did not want to fund a capital project, carry construction risk, and then operate a power asset for twenty years.

The company was deliberately not set up as an EPC contractor or a panel seller. It was set up to hold the asset-owner layer — which meant starting from the questions that decide whether these projects happen at all: how does a project get financed, how is its performance underwritten, who holds the asset, and what has to be true for the numbers to work over two decades of real operation?

The answer was to take ownership. Netso develops the project, arranges the financing, builds the system, operates it for the term, and sells the customer the electricity instead of the equipment. The company’s focus on Bangladesh’s industrial ecosystem follows from that: these are the facilities with the roofs, the loads and the energy costs that make a project viable, and the owners who understand infrastructure decisions.

Tazwar Mahtab founded Netso Energy Ltd. and leads it as Founder & CEO. The company is based in Dhaka and works across Dhaka and Chattogram.

Its first contracted project is an 80 kWp rooftop system at Chittagong Grammar School — a Letter of Intent executed in September 2026 followed by a signed Power Purchase Agreement, with construction to follow. From there Netso is developing a first close of two assets and a longer list of Chattogram sites, one roof at a time.
