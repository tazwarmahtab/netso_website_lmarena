01

Develop

We find out whether the roof can carry a project — technically, commercially and contractually.

Development is where most distributed solar projects are won or lost. A roof that looks promising from the ground may be shaded, structurally inadequate, or attached to a load that does not match the generation profile. We test all of it before anyone commits capital.

  • Site and roof survey, including structure, access and obstructions
  • Load analysis from consumption data and tariff structure
  • Generation modelling against the facility’s daytime demand
  • Technical feasibility, layout and indicative yield
  • Commercial structuring: tariff, term, obligations, risk allocation
  • Internal investment case and approval to proceed
02

Finance + build

Netso funds the project and delivers it. The customer’s balance sheet is not part of the construction.

Project capital is arranged by Netso and held in the project company that owns the asset. That structure matters: the investment sits against a contracted revenue stream and a physical asset, which is what makes distributed generation financeable at scale in this market.

  • Project company formation and capital structure
  • Financing: equity plus project or debt facilities as appropriate
  • Detailed engineering and equipment specification
  • Procurement, contracting and construction management
  • Grid connection, protection and metering
  • Testing, commissioning and handover into operation
03

Own + operate

The asset belongs to Netso. So does everything that keeps it producing.

For the whole term, the system is monitored, maintained and insured by the party that owns it. Performance is measured against the generation the project was underwritten on, and Netso carries the consequence of underperformance.

  • 24-hour monitoring of generation, availability and faults
  • Preventive maintenance, cleaning and corrective response
  • Inverter replacement and lifecycle component management
  • Insurance, statutory compliance and reporting
  • Performance reporting to the customer and to project lenders
  • Asset-level record keeping for the life of the agreement
04

Buy the power

The customer buys kilowatt-hours, not equipment.

A Power Purchase Agreement sets out what is bought and sold: the tariff per unit, the term, how the electricity is measured, and what each side is responsible for. From the facility’s point of view, generating electricity on your own roof behaves like a supply contract — predictable, metered, and directly comparable with what you pay today.

The unit of exchange is a kWh, measured at your meter.

Tariff
A contracted rate per kWh for generated electricity
Term
Long-term, typically structured around the life of the asset
Measurement
Metered generation delivered to the facility
Customer obligation
Provide site access and consume or purchase the contracted output
Netso obligation
Deliver the asset and keep it producing to the agreed standard
Before any of that Feasibility

How we decide whether a roof can support a project.

A viable project needs three things to line up: a roof that can carry the system, a load that can absorb the generation, and an owner willing to commit to a long-term supply agreement.

We assess the first two ourselves. If a site does not work, we say so early — a project that cannot be financed and operated for twenty years is not worth developing for either side.

Roof area & layout
Usable surface, orientation, slope, obstructions, access
Structure
Load-bearing capacity and condition of the roof structure
Consumption
Annual and daytime load, load profile, seasonality
Tariff
Current unit cost of grid electricity at the facility
Sanctioned load
Contracted capacity, and how onsite generation interacts with it
Existing systems
Any solar or backup generation already installed
Horizon
Ownership or lease length, and any planned relocation or expansion

What we need from you to start

  • Approximate rooftop area and building type
  • Recent electricity consumption, or the monthly bill
  • Your current tariff, if you know it
  • Sanctioned load, if you know it
  • Who owns the site, and for how long

If you do not have all of it, send what you have. The assessment begins with the facility, not with paperwork.
