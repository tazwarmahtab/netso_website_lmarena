01 — The rooftop opportunity Bangladesh · C&I

Most people see a roof. Netso sees an energy asset.

Bangladesh’s industrial economy runs on large, flat, structurally simple roofs. Almost all of that surface does nothing.

Meanwhile the businesses underneath it pay for electricity that keeps getting more expensive, more carbon-intensive than it needs to be, and less reliable than their operations can afford.

Netso treats the roof as an infrastructure site: a place where an energy asset can be built, owned and operated for decades, producing electricity for the facility below it.

The facility keeps running exactly as it does today. What changes is who owns the equipment on the roof, who pays for it, and who carries the performance risk.

01

Underused surface

Flat, unshaded industrial roofs across Bangladesh can host megawatts of generation without using a single square metre of additional land.

02

Rising cost of power

For energy-intensive manufacturing, electricity is a direct input cost. Onsite generation competes with the tariff the facility already pays — not with a hypothetical one.

03

Capital constraint

Most facility owners would rather invest in their core business than in a power plant on their roof — even when the system economics are strong.

04

No owner for the asset

The gap is not technology or demand. It is who finances, owns and operates the system over its full life. That is the layer Netso occupies.

02 — What Netso does One partner, whole asset life

Develop. Finance. Own. Operate.

Netso is not an installer and not an equipment retailer. We build the energy asset on your roof, hold it, and sell your facility the power it produces.

01 · Develop

Find and structure the project

Site assessment, load analysis, feasibility and commercial structuring — before any capital is committed.

02 · Finance

Fund the infrastructure

Netso and its project financing partners fund the system inside a project company that owns the asset. The facility is not asked to fund the build.

03 · Own + operate

Hold and run the asset

The system sits with its owner. Monitoring, maintenance and insurance stay with Netso for the life of the agreement.

04 · You buy the power

Pay per kWh

Your facility buys the generated electricity under a long-term PPA — an operating cost, not a capital project.

The financing structure varies — the principle does not

Exact terms are project-specific: they depend on the facility, the load, the counterparty and the capital providers involved. What is consistent is who carries the asset. Netso develops the project, arranges the financing for it, owns the system, and is paid for the electricity it delivers rather than for installing it.

03 — The model You buy electricity, not equipment

You use the power. We build and operate the asset.

  1. 01

    Site

    You have a facility with roof area and an electricity load. We assess both.

  2. 02

    Contract

    We agree a long-term PPA: a tariff per kWh, a term, and the obligations on both sides.

  3. 03

    Investment

    Netso funds the project through its financing structure. No capital expenditure is required from you.

  4. 04

    Build

    Engineering, procurement, construction and commissioning — managed by Netso.

  5. 05

    Operate

    We monitor, maintain, insure and report on the asset for the term of the agreement.

  6. 06

    Bill

    You are billed for the electricity delivered, measured at the meter — like any other utility.

The roof becomes an energy-generating asset that creates economic value — for the business below it and for the investors who own it.

What we do not claim

Netso does not claim that a roof produces revenue on its own. Value appears where a project is viable and a PPA is signed: metered electricity is sold to the facility at a contracted rate, and the asset that produces it is owned and operated by Netso or the project company that holds it.

05 — Why Netso is different Developer, not vendor

Infrastructure, not installation.

The same roof, two completely different commercial structures. One is a purchase. The other is an energy relationship.

Conventional solar purchase

The customer buys a system

  • Customer buys the system
  • Customer funds the capital cost
  • Customer owns the equipment
  • Customer manages operations and maintenance
  • A one-off installation
Netso

The customer buys the power

  • Netso develops the asset
  • Financed at project level — the structure varies by project
  • Netso or the project company owns the asset
  • Netso operates and maintains it, and carries performance risk
  • A long-term energy relationship under a PPA

Financing structures vary by project — the mix of equity and debt, the tenor, and the security package are agreed case by case. The distinction that does not vary: in this model the customer buys energy, and Netso owns and operates the infrastructure that makes it.

06 — Who we build for Facility types

Any facility with a large roof and a real electricity bill.

Our customers are commercial and industrial electricity consumers. They tend to be energy-intensive, capital-disciplined, and long-term owners of their sites.

Garment & textile manufacturing Industrial factories Warehouses & logistics Commercial buildings Schools & institutions Industrial estates

The decision-makers we work with

Managing directors and owners weighing an operating cost against a capital project. CFOs who need the numbers to be defensible. Operations and facilities leaders who need the roof to keep working. Energy and sustainability leads asked to cut consumption without slowing the operation.

The conversation is commercial and technical, not aspirational. We are not selling a symbol of sustainability; we are proposing infrastructure that produces cheaper electricity from a surface you already own.

What a good site looks like

Roof
Large, flat or low-slope, structurally sound, with manageable obstructions
Load
Daytime consumption that the generation can be consumed behind the meter
Tariff
A commercial or industrial tariff high enough for onsite generation to compete
Horizon
Ownership or a lease long enough to support a multi-decade agreement
07 — Development portfolio Pipeline — not operational

The first asset is a reference point, not the business.

We publish the stage and the capacity of every project we are developing. Counterparties are named only where an agreement is in place. No project is described as built, commissioned or generating until it is.

09 — About Netso

We are building the asset-owner layer for distributed C&I solar in Bangladesh.

Netso Energy is a project developer and asset owner, not an EPC contractor, equipment retailer or sustainability consultancy. We find commercial rooftops with viable energy economics, develop the project, arrange the capital, build the system, and hold it for the term of the agreement.

The company was founded on a specific conviction: the constraint on distributed energy in Bangladesh is not technology and not demand — it is ownership. Someone has to be willing to put capital into an asset on someone else’s roof and be accountable for it for twenty years. That is the job Netso does.
